WYSIWYG

Michael Neff  ·  2026

The address of the image above never changes. The image does.

Hotlinking is the practice of displaying an image from someone else's server on your own page. The convention is that the borrower controls the page and the host controls the file — so the host can change what appears, at any time, without touching the page it appears on. Usually this is an accident, a prank, or a bandwidth dispute. Here it is the work.

This listing shows one image from a set of 1,373: broken-image placeholders collected from around the web, interleaved with generated photographs of demolition. A new one takes over every ninety seconds. Nothing about the listing changes. No two people arriving at different moments are looking at the same object.

What is being sold

An image on a web page is fetched once, when the page loads, and never updates afterward. So whatever arrived when you opened this listing stays on your screen while you read, decide, and check out. The server, meanwhile, has moved on. By the time a purchase completes, the object recorded by the transaction is not the object the buyer was looking at when they chose to buy it.

Every request for the image is logged. At the moment of sale both images are recorded — the one on the buyer's screen and the one the transaction timestamps — and this listing is frozen permanently on the second. What you receive is fixed by the purchase, not by the looking.

The buyer receives the frozen image as an archival print, together with the server log of every view this listing received and the record identifying the two images at the moment of sale.

Exhibition

Presented as part of WYSIWYG, an exhibition of artists working with technology and media in ways that reveal how digital ephemera are embodied in material and infrastructure — staged entirely within eBay listings.
